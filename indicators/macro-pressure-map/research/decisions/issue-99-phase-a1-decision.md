# Issue #99 A1 finding

## Verdict

`effr_measurement_not_the_main_problem`

The SF Fed Proxy Funds Rate confirms that broader monetary-policy stance differs materially from EFFR after 2008, especially around the pandemic.

But replacing EFFR with the Proxy Funds Rate does **not** rescue the Issue #97 direction/speed model.

### Full common OOS sample, 1990-01 to 2025-06

EFFR:
- M1 RMSE 1.163
- M2 RMSE 1.204
- M2 − M1 = **+0.041** (worse)
- MAE also worsens by +0.030

Proxy Funds Rate:
- M1 RMSE 1.184
- M2 RMSE 1.304
- M2 − M1 = **+0.120** (worse)
- MAE worsens by +0.075

### Post-December-2008 unconventional-policy period

Proxy M2 versus M1:
- RMSE **+0.132**
- MAE **+0.068**

Direction/speed therefore does not become stable merely because the policy target includes forward guidance and balance-sheet effects.

### Pandemic / post-pandemic

Proxy direction metrics improve somewhat with M2, but RMSE and MAE still worsen. This repeats the pattern seen in Issue #97: recent directional usefulness does not generalize into a stable continuous policy-reaction model.

### Interpretation

The policy measurement issue is real:
- Proxy and EFFR are almost identical on average before December 2008.
- Their average absolute gap becomes larger after 2008 and exceeds 1 percentage point in the 2020-2025 segment.

But this measurement difference is **not** the main reason the simple linear acceleration model failed.

The next hypothesis should therefore be structural:

> direction and speed probably matter conditionally on the macro state, rather than with one constant linear coefficient across all inflation/growth environments.

Treasury outcomes remain unopened.
